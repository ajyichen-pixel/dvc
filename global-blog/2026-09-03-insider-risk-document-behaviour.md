# Insider Risk Without Guesswork: Read the Document Behaviour Before You Judge the Person

**Published:** 2026-09-03  
**DVC Global Security Insights**

Internal data incidents range from simple mistakes to deliberate theft. A user may send a file to the wrong recipient, move work to a personal cloud drive, upload customer information to a public AI service, access unusually large volumes of engineering data before leaving the company, or overshare documents with a supplier. The visible action may look similar while the business context is completely different.

## Core principle

Do not judge a user from one event. Combine document sensitivity, access volume, time, destination, channel and change from normal behaviour before deciding how serious the risk is.

## Seven practical controls

### 1. Start with document value
Public brochures are not equivalent to CAD, BOM, price lists, contracts, customer records or R&D material. Higher sensitivity deserves stronger controls around copying, uploading and external transmission.

### 2. Look for change, not only clicks
A person who normally touches a few files but suddenly accesses hundreds may have a legitimate project reason or may need review. Comparing activity to a normal baseline reduces false positives.

### 3. Give each channel a different risk meaning
Corporate email, approved supplier portals, personal email, USB, public cloud and public AI services are not equivalent. DLP should be able to block, warn, allow or audit according to destination and business purpose.

### 4. Make approved AI safer
Where AI has a valid business purpose, approved workflows can automatically de-identify names, email addresses, phone numbers and customer identifiers before processing. Highly confidential drawings or regulated data can still be blocked.

### 5. Keep control after external sharing
Documents sent to partners can retain expiry, view/download/print permissions, watermarking and central revocation. This extends insider-risk governance through the full document lifecycle instead of ending at Send.

### 6. Preserve explainable evidence
Who touched which document, when, through what channel, to what destination, which rule triggered and whether the result was allow, warn or block should be auditable.

### 7. Design for early correction
Warnings, automated de-identification, secure external sharing, encryption and revocation can redirect risky behaviour before sensitive information becomes uncontrolled.

## Two enterprise scenarios

**Pre-departure bulk access:** an employee nearing departure accesses a large volume of engineering drawings late at night and attempts to upload them to a personal cloud account. High sensitivity + unusual volume + external destination creates a stronger risk signal than any one event.

**Approved AI workflow:** a service team sends customer material into an approved AI workflow. DVC de-identifies personal identifiers before processing so the business task can continue with lower exposure.

DVC combines persistent document protection and encryption, DLP, automated de-identification, secure external sharing, watermarking, access control, expiry/revocation and audit visibility across AI, cloud and external channels. The goal is to manage insider risk as explainable document behaviour rather than guesswork about people.
