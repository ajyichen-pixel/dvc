# Insider Risk Before the Leak: Why Critical Project Folder Activity Is a Security Signal

**DVC Global Security Insights — 27 August 2026**

Data leakage often begins before a file is uploaded, attached to an email, or moved to a cloud service. Earlier signals may include sudden access to high-value project folders, rapid copying of engineering drawings or contracts, batch renaming, archive creation, or activity outside a user's normal role and working pattern.

## DLP is essential, but the precursor matters

DLP controls the moment when data attempts to leave through email, browsers, cloud storage, USB, AI services, or collaboration applications. Critical-folder activity adds the context that comes before that moment.

**Signals worth correlating:** access spikes; users outside the expected project team; rapid copying of CAD, BOM, quotations or customer specifications; archive creation; high-volume after-hours activity; and AI/browser/chat/cloud uploads immediately afterwards.

## Focus on high-value repositories

A proportionate insider-risk programme does not need to watch every action by every employee. Start with repositories containing high-value intellectual property, define normal access by role, and investigate meaningful deviations. In manufacturing, these repositories often contain CAD, BOM, process parameters, cost data, prototypes and customer specifications.

## Five practical controls

1. Define critical projects and folders.
2. Baseline normal access patterns by role and team.
3. Correlate copy, move and archive activity with DLP events.
4. Preserve evidence when sensitive rules trigger.
5. Keep encryption, access rights, expiry, revocation and watermarking attached to important files.

The goal is not employee surveillance. The goal is earlier detection of unusual movement around high-value information using transparent, scoped rules and evidence that supports responsible investigation.
