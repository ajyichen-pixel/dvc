# After the Supplier Handoff: How to Keep CAD, BOM and Engineering Files Under Control

**Published:** 28 August 2026  
**DVC Global Security Insights**

Manufacturing collaboration requires sensitive files to leave the enterprise. CAD drawings, BOMs, tooling files, process sheets, quotations and customer specifications routinely move to suppliers and contractors. The security question is not only whether the transfer is encrypted, but whether control survives after the recipient receives a copy.

## Secure transport is only the first half

TLS, VPNs and secure file-transfer portals protect data in transit. They do not automatically prevent a downloaded copy from being retained, forwarded, printed or reused after a project ends.

DLP and persistent document protection therefore solve different stages of the same workflow. DLP governs the moment information attempts to leave through email, browsers, cloud storage, USB or applications. Document-level controls continue after the handoff through access rights, expiry, revocation, watermarking and audit visibility.

## A six-layer supplier-sharing model

1. Classify the sensitivity of the file and project.
2. Verify the intended supplier, recipient and destination.
3. Apply DLP policy to the outbound channel.
4. Attach document-level access rights before release.
5. Set an expiry period and retain the ability to revoke access centrally.
6. Use watermarking and audit evidence to improve accountability.

## Engineering IP needs purpose-based controls

CAD and BOM data often represent intellectual property, manufacturing know-how or customer-confidential information. Highly sensitive engineering files should not be uploaded to public AI services merely for convenience. For approved AI workflows, limit input to the minimum data required and keep controlled originals separate from working copies.

Access should match the real business window — quotation, prototyping, production ramp-up or contract term — rather than remain open indefinitely. When a project closes, a supplier changes or responsibilities move, central revocation can reduce the risk of old copies remaining usable without limit.

## The goal is controlled collaboration, not blocked collaboration

Supply chains cannot operate if every external file transfer is prohibited. A stronger model combines DLP at the boundary with controls that remain attached to important documents after they leave the organisation.
