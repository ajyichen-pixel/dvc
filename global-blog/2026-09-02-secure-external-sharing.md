# SENT, NOT SURRENDERED: Secure External Sharing After the File Leaves

**Published: 2026-09-02**  
**Topic: Enterprise document security · secure external sharing · supplier security · DLP**

Enterprises cannot operate without sharing sensitive information. Procurement sends CAD drawings for quotation. Engineering sends specifications and BOMs to subcontractors. Sales sends price sheets and contracts. Quality teams exchange reports, and project teams share customer information with external partners.

The business need is legitimate. The security problem is what happens **after** the file leaves the internal repository.

A conventional email attachment or ordinary download link solves delivery. It may not solve these questions:

- Who can still open the document after it is downloaded?
- How long should the recipient retain access?
- Can the document be downloaded, printed, copied or forwarded?
- Can an obsolete revision or wrongly addressed document be stopped later?
- Can the enterprise prove who opened the file and when?

## Seven controls for secure external sharing

### 1. View without distributing an unrestricted original

A supplier reviewing a CAD drawing for quotation may not need a permanent local copy. Secure web viewing, identity verification, opening limits and time-bound access can let the supplier work while reducing uncontrolled copies.

### 2. Bind access to the project lifecycle

Quotation, prototype, production, inspection and contract stages all have natural end points. A document that is legitimate to use today should not automatically remain usable one year later. Access can expire when the business reason expires.

### 3. Separate view, download, print and copy permissions

“Authorised to read” does not need to mean “authorised to take an unrestricted copy.” A partner can be allowed to view necessary information while download, printing or copying remains restricted.

### 4. Use dynamic watermarking for accountability

User identity, company, IP address, time and file name can be displayed on screen or output. This improves deterrence and traceability for CAD drawings, BOMs, price lists, contracts and customer records.

### 5. Make post-send revocation a normal control

Wrong recipient, obsolete design revision, supplier change, employee departure or contract termination can all require immediate access withdrawal. Asking the recipient to delete a copy is not equivalent to centrally stopping future access.

### 6. Keep DLP in front of the sharing workflow

A secure destination does not mean every file is approved for release. DLP can evaluate the user, file sensitivity, content, destination and transfer channel before deciding whether to block, warn, allow or record the action.

### 7. Audit the entire lifecycle, not only the transfer

Useful evidence connects sender, recipient, file, time, IP address, opening activity, download/print rights, expiry and revocation. That complete story is much more useful during an incident than a single “message sent” record.

## Two real-world business scenarios

**Machining quotation:** Procurement shares a confidential CAD drawing with a machining supplier. The supplier receives view-only access for 14 days with a dynamic watermark. Download and print are disabled. When the quotation period ends, access expires automatically.

**Wrong recipient:** A sales user sends a confidential price sheet to the wrong company. The administrator revokes access centrally so the recipient cannot continue opening the document.
