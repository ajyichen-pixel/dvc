# Secure External Sharing: Why Encryption Alone Is Not Enough After a File Leaves Your Company

**Published:** 30 August 2026  
**DVC Global Security Insights**

Enterprises routinely send contracts, quotations, drawings, reports, customer specifications and project files to suppliers, partners and customers. Encryption protects a file during transfer and at rest, but the business risk does not end when the recipient downloads it. Once an ordinary copy is outside the organization, it may be forwarded, retained beyond the project, printed, copied into another system or opened by someone who was never part of the original workflow.

## Secure external sharing needs controls that survive the handoff

A practical document-security model combines boundary controls with persistent document controls. DLP can inspect or govern the moment a file attempts to leave through email, browsers, cloud services, USB or selected applications. Persistent protection then addresses the period after delivery: who can open the file, for how long, whether access can be revoked, what watermark appears, and what activity can be audited.

This distinction matters because transfer security answers **how the file travels**, while persistent document security answers **what can happen after it arrives**.

## Five controls for safer external collaboration

1. **Verify destination and purpose.** Confirm the intended company, recipient, project and business reason before release.
2. **Apply DLP before transmission.** Detect sensitive keywords, file categories or policy conditions before a document leaves a controlled endpoint.
3. **Limit the access window.** Use an expiry date that matches the quotation, review, prototype, production or contract period instead of leaving access open indefinitely.
4. **Keep a revocation path.** If a project ends, a supplier changes or a recipient is no longer authorized, centrally disabling future access can reduce exposure from old copies.
5. **Add accountability.** Visible watermarking and audit records can make external handling easier to trace and review.

## Why this is especially important for manufacturing and project work

External collaboration is unavoidable in manufacturing, engineering, construction, professional services and supply chains. CAD drawings, BOMs, process documents, customer requirements and commercial terms often have to leave the company to keep a project moving. Blocking every transfer is not realistic. The better goal is to make necessary sharing controlled, time-bounded and reviewable.

## DLP and controlled external sharing solve different parts of the same problem

DLP is strongest at the decision point before data leaves. Controlled external sharing is strongest after an approved release. Used together, they create a clearer lifecycle: inspect → approve → protect → share → monitor → expire or revoke.
