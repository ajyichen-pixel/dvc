# After CAD Leaves Engineering: 7 Controls for Manufacturing IP

**DVC Global Security Insights — 1 September 2026**

Manufacturing intellectual property lives in CAD drawings, 3D models, BOMs, tooling designs, process parameters, part specifications and supplier versions. Once those files are exported from PDM, PLM or engineering repositories and move through email, cloud storage, USB, collaboration tools, suppliers or generative AI, repository permissions alone are no longer enough.

The security question changes after export: **can the organisation still control who opens the file, how long it remains usable, whether it may be forwarded or printed, whether it can be sent to AI, and whether access can later be revoked?**

## 1. Make protection travel with the file

Persistent encryption and rights enforcement keep engineering information protected when it is copied to a desktop, USB drive or external device. A file leaving a shared folder should not automatically become an uncontrolled file.

## 2. Separate viewing, editing, printing and onward sharing

Engineering may need editing rights, procurement may only need viewing, while suppliers may receive time-limited access. Viewing, editing, printing, copying, forwarding and expiry should be treated as separate business permissions.

## 3. Apply DLP across the channels engineering teams actually use

DWG, DXF, STEP, IGES, JT, drawing PDFs and BOMs can leave through browsers, email, cloud sync, USB and messaging apps. Useful DLP evaluates file type, sensitivity, user, channel, destination and action together rather than relying on keywords alone.

## 4. Share with suppliers without surrendering permanent control

Quotation, prototyping, machining and inspection require external collaboration. Expiry, identity checks, opening limits, IP conditions, download or print restrictions, watermarking and revocation let external partners work while the owner retains control. When a design version becomes obsolete or a supplier changes, access should be removable without depending on the recipient to delete copies.

## 5. Use watermarking for accountability

User, company, IP address, time and file name can be shown on screen or output to improve traceability and deterrence. Watermarking complements encryption; it does not replace it.

## 6. Treat generative AI as a separate destination

Public AI, approved enterprise AI and internal AI should not receive identical policy treatment. Confidential CAD may be blocked from public services, while approved workflows can de-identify customer names, emails, phone numbers and other identifiers before processing.

## 7. Audit the complete event story

Useful audit evidence connects the user, file, time, destination, transfer channel, policy decision, opening activity and later revocation. That event story is far more valuable than a single download log when investigating insider risk or supply-chain leakage.
